This is a status page, not a subsystem page: it keeps the usual headings, but "How it works" is the dependency graph of the work, "Interfaces and APIs" lists the interfaces each open or planned change adds, and "Algorithms and processing" is the procedure that takes a change to a radio. A few terms recur; each is also in [17 Glossary](17-glossary.md). **CW** is Morse code sent by switching a carrier on and off. **Keying** a radio makes it transmit, through its **key line** (the contact that keys CW) or its **PTT** (push-to-talk) line. **CI-V** is Icom's serial command protocol, which `hfnode` uses over the IC-7300's USB cable. The **keyer box** is a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 (a small board with the RP2350 microcontroller) that closes a radio's key line; **core 1** is the RP2350's second processor core. A **UF2** file is the firmware image format the Pico's boot loader takes. **rustos** is Robin's bare-metal Rust OS for the Pico 2, which the box firmware is built on. The **UV-K1** is a Quansheng handheld radio, keyed here on NR7Y's CW firmware. A **bench stage** is one step of the hardware bring-up, run with Robin (the project owner) at the radio. **H1** is the safety audit's rubric criterion for measured hardware results (the bench results); unattended use is graded only once they exist and the other H criteria, H2 to H7, are met as well (`audit/rubric.md` §H, project files). The audit's report also numbers its IC-7300 hardware open items H1 to H11; this page writes those as **§4 H1 to H11** to keep them apart from the rubric criterion. The **Po meter** is the radio's output-power meter; **SWR** says how well the antenna takes the power. A **dummy load** is a resistor that takes a transmitter's power in place of an antenna, so nothing goes on the air. A **sidetone** is the tone a radio plays in its headphones while it sends CW; **break-in** lets the radio switch itself to transmit when keyed, and its **delay** is how long it stays on transmit after the last element. The **exciter** is the transmitter's low-power stage. **GP26** is the Pico pin whose ADC (analog-to-digital converter) samples a radio's audio in the decoder images; **DMA** is hardware that copies data, here the ADC's samples, without the processor. **dBFS** is decibels relative to the loudest sample the audio input can hold. **Squelch** is the decoder's threshold for ignoring noise. An **SDR** is a software-defined radio, a wideband receiver whose filtering is done in software. In CI-V, **`1C 00`** reads whether the radio is transmitting and **`17`** tells it to send a text as CW. A **mutant** is a deliberately broken copy of the code that a test must reject; the **mutation scripts** build and test them. A **zero-RF** stage is one in which nothing can transmit. **FCC Part 97** is the US amateur-radio rules; its **automatic control** provisions govern a station that transmits with no operator present.

## How it works

The work falls into three streams that meet at one review.

1. **Merging what is written.** Four pull requests are open. One (#27) is independent. The rest are a chain: #30 (the decoder box) must merge before #31 (the decoder on the keyer box's core 1, stacked on #30), and rustos #6 (starting core 1) must merge before #31 moves its rustos pin to rustos `master`. Moving the pin makes #31 a new head, which the safety audit must clear again with a new hash before anything is flashed. After #31 merges, the cleared image is flashed on Robin's go and the UV-K1's audio is wired into the box.
2. **The hardware bring-up.** Two benches: the IC-7300 (stage 2 of 13 sent to Robin; nothing connected yet) and the UV-K1 on the keyer box (box flashed with today's keyer image `eef8c126` and checked; nothing keyed). Neither bench's first pass needs an open pull request; only the UV-K1's re-run of stages 5 and 6 with its audio wired into GP26 waits on #31. Each bench stage runs on Robin's go, and the results go to the audit.
3. **What unattended use waits on.** The audit grades unattended use only once measured bench results (H1) exist, together with the other H criteria (`audit/rubric.md` §H). It also asks for a hardware timer that ends a stuck transmission without software, and for the IC-7300's link-loss test. The timer does not exist yet, and the IC-7300 steps that need a dummy load or a wattmeter are off the current bench (WL21). The amateur-radio side is settled: Robin accepted the Part 97 risk on 2026-10-08 (see [Decided, not open](#decided-not-open)).

Two planned changes (PR 4 and PR 5 of the decoder plan) hang off #30 and do not block the bench.

### PR #27: front and microphone key preflight (open, not on main)

The preflight (the read-only checks `hfnode` runs before it writes to or keys the IC-7300) gains seven reads and no writes. A front-panel or microphone key set to a "Voice/Keyer/RTTY Memory" sends that memory when pushed; the microphone's UP/DN keys act as a CW key when "MIC Up/Down Keyer" is ON. Either fails `radio tune`, `radio cw` and `run`, and only warns for `radio check` and `radio setup`. The settings are read once when a command starts, not again before each tune ([07 CI-V and the IC-7300](07-civ-ic7300.md)).

## Algorithms and processing

### The merge order

The order below comes from the project's notes and the comment in [`firmware/pico2-keyer/Cargo.toml:29`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/bc5062ae4f56ae1f9949b3b067218c7e675d88f8/firmware/pico2-keyer/Cargo.toml#L29) (PR #31). Each step waits on the one before; every flash waits on Robin's go.

1. **#27** merges whenever Robin chooses; nothing waits on it and it waits on nothing.
2. **#30** merges.
3. **rustos #6** merges.
4. **#31** moves its rustos pin from `410dffa` to rustos `master`'s merge of #6, in `firmware/pico2-keyer/Cargo.toml` and its `Cargo.lock` (WL4), and its base moves to `main`. The new head goes to the audit for a fresh review and a new hash (the cleared `bc5062ae` file stops being the one to flash).
5. **#31** is marked ready and merges.
6. `hfnode` is rebuilt from `main` (the old one refuses protocol 5).
7. On Robin's go, the newly cleared file is flashed. Set `[keyer] firmware_build` in the bench config to the cleared build id (the new id after the re-pin), and back to `eef8c126` if falling back: with it set, `hfnode` refuses a box that reports any other build ([`crates/hfnode/src/keyer.rs:441-451`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/crates/hfnode/src/keyer.rs#L441-L451); the bench config sets it). Then `hfnode keyer check` twice ([`docs/keyer.md:141-150`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/bc5062ae4f56ae1f9949b3b067218c7e675d88f8/docs/keyer.md#L141-L150), PR #31). Later, as a keyed bench stage where `hangtest` runs, under the audit's on-air ruling and on Robin's go: `core1test` and `core1test --fault` (bring-up step 11; they identify, so they key the radio; `audit/report.md` §3, PR #31 checks).
8. The UV-K1's audio is wired into GP26 as the audit's ruling specifies (`audit/req-handheld-nr7y-cw-ruling.md` §2 and `bench/k1-audio-later.svg`, project files; summarised on [11 Decoder box and the one-Pico image](11-decoder-box-one-pico.md)), and UV-K1 stages 5 and 6 are run again.

In parallel, after step 2: PR 4 (the patch applied on a branch from `main`, WL7), then PR 5 with its audit. After PR 4 and step 5: `source = "keyer"`, with its own audit review. Neither bench's first pass needs any of this: the IC-7300 bench needs no open PR, and the UV-K1 bench runs on today's `eef8c126` image.

Trial merges with `git merge-tree` for this page found no conflicts for `main` + #27, `main` + #31 (which carries #30), and #27 + #31. That is a textual check only; CI on the real merge is what counts.

### Why a re-pin or a merge makes a new file

The box firmware stamps the first eight characters of its commit into its `HELLO` reply as its build id ([`firmware/pico2-keyer/build.sh:21-22`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/firmware/pico2-keyer/build.sh#L21-L22), [`firmware/pico2-keyer/src/main.rs:95-101`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/firmware/pico2-keyer/src/main.rs#L95-L101)), so every commit builds to a different UF2. Separately, moving the rustos pin "can change the UF2 with no source change, since cargo mixes the commit into the crate hashes that order the code" ([`firmware/pico2-keyer/Cargo.toml:24-28`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/bc5062ae4f56ae1f9949b3b067218c7e675d88f8/firmware/pico2-keyer/Cargo.toml#L24-L28), PR #31). The project learned this from an earlier pin move: every keyer build printed an "invalid character `{` in package name" error from rustos's `templates/pico2/Cargo.toml`; rustos #4 (merged 2026-10-09) fixed it and this repository's #24 moved the pin. The audit confirmed that `eef8c126` still builds byte for byte to the cleared file (`audit/report.md` §3, "Firmware pin bump PR #24"), but the project's notes record that pinning #4's branch commit, the same tree, moved 46,030 bytes of code (memory note `rustos-template-cargo-error.md`). So the cleared files are exact files from named CI runs, and `main`'s merge commit of a PR builds a file that was never cleared. The procedure is on [14 Audit, bench and flashing](14-audit-bench-flashing.md).

### Triage of a red CI run

The CI runs the mock IC-7300 scenarios at 20 times real time ([`.github/workflows/ci.yml:16`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/.github/workflows/ci.yml#L16)) and the keyer scenarios at up to 5 times ([`crates/hfnode/src/selftest/any_radio.rs:48`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/crates/hfnode/src/selftest/any_radio.rs#L48)), on wall clocks, so a hosted runner that pauses the test process can fail a scenario. The project handles that without loosening anything:

1. Find the test and the operating system. Most known flakes are on macOS (see [Flakes](#flakes)).
2. Read the pause measurements. Scenarios record the longest whole-process pause in a `machine` check; a run paused past its limit (`PAUSE_LIMIT`, 1 s of radio time for keyer scenarios, [`any_radio.rs:55`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/crates/hfnode/src/selftest/any_radio.rs#L55)) is not judged and runs again, up to `NOT_JUDGED_RERUNS = 2` times ([`crates/hfnode/src/selftest.rs:1570`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/crates/hfnode/src/selftest.rs#L1570)).
3. A lone macOS keyer failure that matches a known flake gets one re-run (the project's rule).
4. A failure that repeats, or a new signature, is a bug until shown otherwise. Windows IC-7300 scenario failures that looked like flakes (`pending-timeout`, `lost-result`, `rx-station-id`, `rx-several`, `fault-stuck-last-over`) were a real ordering bug: a held-up duty-file write was timed as keying. It was fixed in PR #21, with follow-ups in PR #22 (the project's notes; `audit/report.md`, the #21 and #22 reviews).
5. Never widen a timeout, lower the 20x scale or loosen a limit to make a test pass. **These are never-loosen rules.**

[12 Testing](12-testing.md) has the full flake table and the pause meters.

## Changing it

How to take an item from this page:

1. **Read the subsystem page** the item links to, and the code at the cited lines. Check the item is still open (the commands above; the code may have moved on since 2026-10-13).
2. **Send it to the audit when it can matter.** Anything that can change what keys a radio, what is written to a radio, a limit, gate or timeout, or a firmware image goes to the safety audit. The audit has also reviewed test-only and docs-only changes: test-only PR #20 (the handheld's station-ID timing test) and docs PRs #25 and #26 (wiring and bench procedure), `audit/report.md` §3 and its requirements table. It classified two changes as needing no hardware-safety review (#28 and rustos #5, "Classified 2026-10-11 09:10Z"). So send any change to [docs/keyer.md](../keyer.md), [docs/hardware-test-plan.md](../hardware-test-plan.md), the wiring in `docs/decoder.md`, the bench config examples, or tests of keying, limits or gates. Whether a change needs review is the audit's decision, never the author's.
3. **Write the change with its tests.** The tests that apply are listed on [12 Testing](12-testing.md): unit tests in the crate, the mock-radio scenarios (`hfnode selftest`, and the end-to-end tests under `crates/hfnode/tests/`), and, for firmware, the CI checks that the fault handlers open the key first and that core 1 never reaches a pin.
4. **Keep the never-loosen rules.** No limit, gate, timeout or the 20x CI scale is loosened, and no change goes round the audit. The full list is on [13 Safety rules](13-safety-rules.md); the box limits are in [`crates/keyer-core/src/lib.rs:48-94`](https://github.com/robinonsay/cw-message-gateway/blob/44185fa9a00369c379d9d8af40ca307e3867e860/crates/keyer-core/src/lib.rs#L48-L94).
5. **Update the docs** that describe the item, including this page's row.
